Today’s lesson

Learning intentions

  • To be able to understand the mean and the median as measures of centre.
  • To be able to understand the range, interquartile range and standard deviation as measures of spread.
  • To be able to know whether to use the median and interquartile range, or the mean and standard deviation, for a particular distribution.
  • To be able to use a CAS calculator to calculate these summary statistics.

Two questions about any distribution

Where is the centre?

median (M): the middle value

mean (\(\bar x\)): the balance point

How spread out is it?

range: largest − smallest

IQR: spread of the middle 50%

standard deviation (s): spread around the mean

They come in pairs: median with IQR · mean with standard deviation.
Cambridge General Mathematics Units 3&4, 1F pp. 44, 50–52
Learn · 1F

The median: the middle value

The median

The median is the middle value in an ordered data set.
For n data values the median is located at the \(\dfrac{n+1}{2}\)th position. When:

  • n is odd, the median will be the middle data value
  • n is even, the median will be the average of the two middle data values.

The mean is the balance point

\(\bar x=\dfrac{\sum x}{n}\)  \(\sum\) = ‘sum of’, \(x\) = a data value, \(n\) = number of values

Resistant: the mean is affected by changing the largest data value but the median is not. Drag the orange dot to try it.

Worked example 21 · 1F
Calculating the mean from the formula
The following is a set of reaction times (in milliseconds): 38 36 35 43 46 64 48 25
Write down the values of the following, correct to one decimal place.
  1. a\(n\)
  2. b\(\sum x\)
  3. c\(\bar x\)

a \(n\) is the number of data values: \(n=\)8

b \(\sum x=38+36+35+43+46+64+48+25=\)335

c \(\bar x=\dfrac{\sum x}{n}=\dfrac{335}{8}=41.875\) → 41.9

1
Count
\(n\) = how many values.
2
Add
\(\sum x\) = the sum of the values.
3
Divide
\(\bar x=\sum x \div n\), then round.

Learn · 1F

Mean or median?

Symmetric, no outliers

mean ≈ median: either can be used

Clearly skewed and/or outliers

the tail drags the mean: use the median

Choosing between the mean and the median

The mean and the median are both measures of the centre of a distribution. If the distribution is:

  • symmetric and there are no outliers, either the mean or the median can be used to indicate the centre of the distribution
  • clearly skewed and/or there are outliers, it is more appropriate to use the median to indicate the centre of the distribution.
Key box: Cambridge General Mathematics Units 3&4, 1F p. 52 · Sketches: teacher illustration (dashed line = mean)

Standard deviation: spread around the mean

\(s=\sqrt{\dfrac{\sum (x-\bar x)^2}{n-1}}\)

Roughly, the typical distance of the data values from the mean. On CAS it is sx. Same mean, different spread:
