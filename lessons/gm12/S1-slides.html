Today’s lesson

Learning intentions

  • To be able to classify data as categorical or numerical.
  • To be able to further classify categorical data as nominal or ordinal.
  • To be able to further classify numerical data as discrete or continuous.
  • To be able to construct frequency and percentage frequency tables for categorical data.
  • To be able to construct bar charts and percentage bar charts from frequency tables.
  • To be able to interpret and describe frequency tables and bar charts.
  • To be able to answer statistical questions about a categorical variable.

A real VCAA question you can already do

The percentage segmented bar chart provided shows the age (under 55 years, 55 years and over) of visitors at a travel convention, segmented by preferred travel destination (domestic, international).

The variables age (under 55 years, 55 years and over) and preferred travel destination (domestic, international) are
A.both categorical variables.
B.both numerical variables.
C.a numerical variable and a categorical variable respectively.
D.a categorical variable and a numerical variable respectively.
E.a discrete variable and a continuous variable respectively.
Solution · A

Age has been recorded in groups (ordinal) and destination is a name (nominal). Both are categorical.

What’s the distractor?
Distractor · C

Students saw age and assumed it was numerical. Here it has been recorded in groups (under 55, 55 and over), so it is categorical.

Learn · 1A

When you’re not sure: two tests

Test 1 · Can you average it?

It makes sense to calculate the average weight of a group of people, but not the average house number in a street.

Numbers that are just labels are categorical.

Test 2 · How was it recorded?

weight recorded in kilograms → numerical

weight recorded as ‘underweight’, ‘normal weight’, ‘overweight’ → categorical

The variable name alone doesn’t decide.

Worked example 1 · 1A
Types of data
Classify the following data as nominal, ordinal, discrete or continuous.
  1. aThe number of chocolate chips in each of 10 cookies is counted.
  2. bThe time taken for 20 students to complete a puzzle is recorded in seconds.
  3. cMember of a football club were asked to rate how they felt about the current coach, 1 = Very satisfied, 2 = Satisfied, 3 = Indifferent, 4 = Dissatisfied, 5 = Very dissatisfied.
  4. dStudents are asked to each choose their preferred colour from the list 1 = Blue, 2 = Green, 3 = Red, 4 = Yellow.
  5. eStudents weights were classified as ‘less than 60kg’, ‘60kg - 80kg’ or ‘more than 80kg’.

a Discrete, as the number of chocolate chips will only take whole number values.

b Continuous, as the data can take any value, limited only by the accuracy to which the time can be measured.

c Ordinal, as the numbers in this data do not represent quantities, they represent each person’s level of approval of the coach.

d Nominal, as the numbers in this data again do not represent quantities, they represent colours.

e Ordinal, as each student’s weight has been recorded into three categories which can be ordered.

Learn · 1B

From a bar chart to a segmented bar chart

1 · Stack the bars

Same frequencies, one on top of the other. The total height is 23.

2 · Convert to percentages

Each segment becomes its percentage, so the bar is exactly 100% tall.

Why?

One compact bar shows each category’s share of the whole, and makes groups easy to compare.

Describing the distribution of a categorical variable

The mode

The most frequently occurring category: the tallest bar or longest segment.

A report includes

the context and how many
the modal category
percentages for the others

Context: 40 students were asked how they travel to school: car 22 (55%), bus 12 (30%), walk 6 (15%).

The variablemode of travel to school of number40 individualsstudents were classified as being categories‘car’, ‘bus’ or ‘walk’. The majority / mostmajority of the individualsstudents, %55%, were found to modal categorytravel by car. Of the remaining individualsstudents, %30% were found to categorycatch the bus, while %15% were found to categorywalk.

Use ‘majority’ only when the modal category is more than 50%. Otherwise, say ‘the most common’.
